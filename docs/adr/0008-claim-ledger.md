# Verification is a per-block claim ledger: removals claim first, conflicts are loud, residue is always logged

ADR-0001 says the verifier checks that each quote is an exact substring of its block and the renderer uses the located slice. That is one reference at a time. A document's labelling is many references over the same blocks, plus removals, plus whatever the LLM left unmentioned, and the questions that matter are between them: what if a bullet the LLM placed ends in the phone number it also labelled for removal; what if two fields claim the same words; what if a skill the CV really lists twice is quoted twice, or three times; what is left of a block once everything placed and removed is taken out. The verifier answers all of these with one structure: a **claim ledger** per block, a list of `(start, end, claimant)` over the block's raw text, filled in a fixed order and read once at the end.

Matching is on canonicalised text through the offset map `canonicalise_with_offsets` builds as it goes, and slicing is from raw text, so a straight apostrophe in the LLM's quote matches a curly one in the source and the curly one is what reaches the output. There is no case-folding: a quote in the wrong case is not the source's text.

**Removals claim before content.** The ledger is filled in this order and no other: the LLM's removals in schema order, then the regex backstop for emails, phones and URLs over every block, then content in tree-walk order (name, profile, skills, education, experience, certifications, additional; list order within, an entry's fields in schema order), then the heading backstop over blocks with no placed content, then coverage. Because removals are in the ledger before any content arrives, a content claim that overlaps a removal is clipped to what remains: one piece when the removal sits at an edge (c08's phone at the end of a bullet leaves the bullet as a prefix), two pieces when it sits in the middle, which is the only way a **multi-span unit** is ever made. A content claim wholly inside a removal places nothing. Removal therefore wins over placement by construction, as ADR-0004 requires, and the order is a fact of the code, asserted by a unit test that records the stages as they run and by a test that places a content claim over an email with no removal labelled and finds it clipped. A refactor that moved coverage ahead of the backstop, or content ahead of removals, would fail the suite.

**Conflicts are loud.** A content claim that overlaps another content claim is a conflict. The later claimant in tree-walk order is rejected and gets no claim; the earlier keeps what it holds; whatever of the loser's range the winner does not hold stays unclaimed and surfaces as residue. A repeated quote is not a conflict in itself: each claimant takes the next occurrence free of content, so c06's duplicated skill places twice, and a third identical quote against a block with two occurrences is rejected as a conflict with nothing revoked. A quote with no occurrence at all rejects that one leaf and nothing else: the entry survives with a hole rather than disappearing. Every rejection is recorded with its path, block, quote and reason, so a leaf that vanished can be traced to the reference that lost it.

**Residue is not the same as unplaced text.** Coverage runs last, over the finished ledgers, as a flat list of claims that never walks the content tree, so the tree's shape can change without the coverage rule changing with it. Everything unclaimed in a block is **residue**, and all of it is logged, down to a single space, because the promise is that no text disappears untraceably. But not all of it is text. A residue run made only of **separators** (whitespace, list punctuation, dashes, bullet glyphs including Word's Symbol-font ones, quote marks; the set `SEPARATORS` beside the confusable table so every consumer reads one definition) is separator residue: the comma between two placed skills, the space clipped off a bullet beside a removed phone. It is logged and goes no further. Anything else is **unplaced text** and goes to the review appendix verbatim. The ampersand is deliberately not a separator: `&` is a word in "M&S" and in "Research & Development", so `&` standing alone is unplaced text, loud in the appendix, and never silently dropped.

**The heading backstop is a vocabulary, not a heuristic.** The regex backstop removes emails, phones and URLs from every block whatever the LLM did, because removal is defence in depth. `RM_HEADING` has no regex. It has a fixed vocabulary (the four Layouts' headings, common synonyms, document titles such as "Curriculum Vitae"; case-folded; trailing colon stripped; whole block only) applied only to blocks that hold no placed content. Shape heuristics (short, bold, title-case, alone on a line) were rejected because a short bold job title looks exactly like a heading, and the two failure modes are not symmetric: a false removal is silent, a false appendix entry is loud. A heading the vocabulary misses goes to the appendix where a reviewer sees it; a job title the vocabulary wrongly held would be deleted with nothing to see. The vocabulary is therefore deliberately narrow, and sub-headings inside Additional Information ("Languages", "Interests") are not in it, because the prompt calls them content.

**Text-box order is an approximation.** The parser emits a text box at the position of the body child it is anchored in, immediately after that child's own text, and text boxes anchored in the same child in document order. This is the anchor's position, not the box's: Word places a floating box by its own offsets and wrapping, and a box anchored in an early paragraph can be drawn beside a later one, or two boxes anchored in one paragraph can be drawn in either order. Anchor order therefore approximates visual reading order and is not guaranteed to match it. The golden set's text-box Layout anchors each box in an empty paragraph exactly where it is drawn, so the approximation is exact there and the limitation is recorded rather than discovered. It is stated, not mitigated: recovering visual order would mean reading the drawing's geometry, and the pipeline's promise is provenance, not layout fidelity. The same approximation applies to headers and footers, which are emitted after the body although Word draws a header first. The ledger does not depend on block order at all; only the appendix (source order) and the unplaced list do.

**Considered options:** verifying references one at a time with no ledger (rejected: the phone at the end of a placed bullet is either leaked or the whole bullet lost). Letting content claim before removals (rejected: the appendix and the fields become PII leak paths). Revoking both claimants in a conflict (rejected: a hallucinated third quote would pull the two real placements into the appendix). Treating all residue as unplaced text (rejected: every stray comma raises the banner). Shape heuristics for headings (rejected above).

**Consequence:** transform receives a tree whose leaves are Units of one or more Spans, a flat claim list, a removal list under named rules, and residue split into separator and unplaced; the transform log (Run) carries all of it. The oracle tests of the pipeline seam exercise each rule end to end: a missing leaf, an unlocatable quote, a duplicated claim, a labelling failure.
